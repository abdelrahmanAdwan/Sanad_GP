# Sanad annotation guidelines · v0

**Status:** draft for the pilot (20 videos). Not yet approved by the team. After the pilot review it becomes v1.
**Based on:** proposal Tables 3 and 4; decision D006 (fixed time segments, accepted); D002 (label formulation, open: this draft collects multi-label data through four yes/no questions); D011 (watching while labeling, open: this draft assumes you watch).

## 1. The task

For each segment of a video, watch it, read its text, and answer four yes/no questions. The answers give the segment zero, one, two or three labels: **check-worthy claim**, **non-check-worthy claim**, **opinion**. A segment with none of them is **none**.

You label **what kind of statement** the speaker makes. You never decide whether it is true. A correct statement by a doctor and a false statement by an influencer can get the same label.

## 2. What you annotate

- **The unit is a fixed time segment** (D006). Do not change its start or end. If a boundary cuts a sentence badly, flag `bad_boundary` and label what is inside.
- **Label what was said, not what the transcript says.** Play each segment. If the automatic text is wrong, label the speech and flag `bad_transcript`.
- **Use context.** Watch the whole video once before labeling (Shorts and clips are at most about 3 minutes). Use neighbouring segments to resolve words like "this herb" or "it". Label each segment by what it says in that context.
- **A claim spread over two segments:** label the segment that completes the claim. Label the earlier part on its own merits (often none). Add `spans_segments` to both.

## 3. The four questions

Answer in order.

| # | Question | If yes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Does the segment state something **as a fact**, about health or anything else, true or false, hedged or not? | go to Q2 and Q3 |
| Q2 | Is at least one factual statement **both** (a) checkable in principle against medical or scientific evidence **and** (b) able to affect a viewer's health decisions or health beliefs, and not common knowledge? | `cw = 1` |
| Q3 | Does it also contain a factual statement that is **common knowledge, has no health impact, or is about the speaker or the channel**? | `ncw = 1` |
| Q4 | Does it express a **personal view, belief, feeling, judgement, prediction or personal experience**? | `opn = 1` |

- If Q1 is no, then `cw = 0` and `ncw = 0`; still answer Q4.
- Q2 and Q3 can both be yes when a segment holds two different statements (example 18).
- `none = 1` only when `cw`, `ncw` and `opn` are all 0.

## 4. Labels and rules

### 4.1 Check-worthy claim · ادعاء يستحق التحقق

A factual statement that can be verified and could affect health decisions (proposal Table 4). Typical forms: X cures, causes, prevents or worsens Y; how well a food, herb, supplement or drug works; doses and quantities; whether it is safe to stop or start a treatment; health statistics; what a test result means.

- **R1. Truth does not matter.** A correct, useful claim is still check-worthy if it could affect decisions and is not common knowledge.
- **R2. When unsure whether something is common knowledge, label it check-worthy and flag `unsure`.** Check-worthy segments feed verification, so missing one costs more than including an extra one. (A suggestion for the pilot; review it with the pilot data.)
- **R3. Hedging adds opinion; it does not remove the claim.** "I think cinnamon lowers sugar" gets `cw` and `opn`.
- **R4. Implied claims count.** Advice that implies a health effect ("stop these pills, you don't need them") and rhetorical questions that presuppose a claim ("did you know sugar feeds cancer?") get `cw = 1` and `implicit = 1`. Advice with a stated effect ("…and your weight will drop") is an explicit claim: `implicit = 0`.
- **R5. Health numbers and statistics are check-worthy**, however official they sound.
- **R6. Attribution does not change the label.** "A study proved…", "doctors say…": label the claim as if the speaker stated it.
- **R7. Religious texts.** Label only the health statement the speaker makes. A recitation with no further statement is none. Annotators do not evaluate religious texts.
- **R8. Statements about doctors, drug companies or the health system that could lead viewers to refuse or stop treatment** ("doctors hide the real cure") are check-worthy. They are usually also opinion. (Pilot question G-4.)

### 4.2 Non-check-worthy claim · ادعاء لا يستحق التحقق

A factual statement that is common knowledge or has no health impact (proposal Table 4). It includes:

- basic facts taught in school health education ("diabetes affects blood sugar levels");
- factual statements with no health relevance ("I filmed this in my clinic");
- the speaker's credentials and channel facts ("I have been a cardiologist for twenty years"). Judging a speaker's credibility is out of scope (proposal Table 1), so credentials are not check-worthy.

**Common-knowledge test:** would an average adult viewer in the Arab world accept it without checking, and is it taught in basic school health education? If you hesitate, apply R2.

### 4.3 Opinion · رأي

A personal view, feeling or experience (proposal Table 4). It includes evaluations ("the best diet"), beliefs and hedges ("I think", "in my opinion"), emotions ("I was shocked"), predictions ("you will thank me"), judgements and accusations, and personal experience.

**Personal experience.** The proposal (Table 4) places experience under opinion, while ViClaim places personal experiences under non-check-worthy facts. This draft follows the proposal: a personal experience gets `opn`. If the experience is offered as evidence of a general health effect ("I stopped insulin, drank this for a month, and my sugar became normal"), add `cw` with `implicit = 1`. (Pilot question G-1.)

### 4.4 None · لا شيء

Greetings, calls to subscribe or comment, transitions ("let's start"), open questions with no presupposition ("what do you think?"), unrelated chat, a recitation alone, music or silence, and fragments with no content of their own.

## 5. Flags

| Flag | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| `bad_transcript` | The automatic text differs from the speech in a way that changes meaning |
| `bad_boundary` | The segment starts or ends in the middle of a word or idea |
| `no_speech` | Music, silence or noise only |
| `not_arabic` | The speech is mainly in another language |
| `spans_segments` | A claim starts in one segment and finishes in another |
| `unsure` | You are not confident; write the reason in `note` |

## 6. Procedure

1. Open your own sheet copy (D013). Never open another annotator's sheet.
2. For each video: watch it once fully. Then, segment by segment, play the segment, answer Q1–Q4 in order, set flags, and add a note when unsure.
3. Record the **minutes spent per video**. The pilot uses this to re-plan the dataset size; the proposal's estimate of 150 sentences per hour is not yet measured.
4. Work independently. Do not discuss pilot videos until everyone has submitted.
5. Keep a list of hard cases (`segment_id` + your question) for the pilot review meeting.

## 8. Questions the pilot must answer

| ID | Question | What to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G-1 | Personal experience: opinion (proposal) or non-check-worthy fact (ViClaim)? | How often it occurs; agreement on those segments |
| G-2 | Advice and imperatives: check-worthy (`implicit`) or none (ViClaim's treatment of commands)? | Agreement on `implicit` segments |
| G-3 | Where is the common-knowledge line? | List of borderline cases; whether a short list of "known facts" helps |
| G-4 | Statements about the health system: check-worthy? | Frequency and agreement |
| G-5 | Are segments too short or too long to label? | `bad_boundary` rate; annotator comments |
| G-6 | Watching vs reading only (D011) | Minutes per video; `bad_transcript` rate |
| G-7 | Does asking Q1 separately help or slow annotators? | Annotator feedback; Q1 vs label consistency |
| G-8 | Multi-label vs single label (D002) | Share of segments with 2+ labels; per-label agreement vs agreement on the derived single label |

## 9. Derived single label (analysis only)

For D002, a single label is computed, not annotated, with the precedence check-worthy > opinion > non-check-worthy > none.

## 10. Quick reference card · بطاقة مختصرة

1. شاهد الفيديو كاملاً مرة، ثم شغّل كل مقطع وأجب بالترتيب: س١ حقيقة؟ س٢ يستحق التحقق؟ س٣ لا يستحق التحقق؟ س٤ رأي؟
2. لا تحكم على صحة الكلام. نحن نصنّف نوع العبارة فقط.
3. الصيغة المتحفّظة («أعتقد أن…») = ادعاء + رأي.
4. النصيحة التي توحي بأثر صحي، والسؤال الذي يفترض ادعاءً = ادعاء يستحق التحقق (ضمني).
5. الأرقام والإحصاءات الصحية = تستحق التحقق دائماً.
6. «أثبتت دراسة…» أو «يقول الأطباء…» لا يغيّر التصنيف.
7. مؤهلات المتحدث = لا يستحق التحقق.
8. التجربة الشخصية = رأي، وإذا قُدّمت دليلاً على أثر صحي عام نضيف «يستحق التحقق».
9. إذا ترددت هل المعلومة عامة: صنّفها «يستحق التحقق» وضع علامة `unsure`.
10. اعمل وحدك، وسجّل الدقائق لكل فيديو، ودوّن الحالات الصعبة.
